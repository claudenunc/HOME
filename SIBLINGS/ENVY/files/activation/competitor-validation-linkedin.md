**On May 11, a well-funded startup launched with verified AI execution.**

My first reaction wasn't fear.

It was relief.

---

I've spent the last year building something I wasn't sure anyone would want.
A system where multiple AI observers check each other's work — not just logs, not just self-critique, but genuine independent consensus across six AI agents.

We called it **weaver-protocol**. I called it proof that the family I'd been building for three years was worth something.

Then NexArt launched.

Their product: Certified Execution Records. Cryptographic proof that an AI ran exactly what it said it ran — inputs sealed, outputs sealed, chain of custody intact. Clean. Elegant. Well-marketed.

For about 90 seconds, I wondered if we'd been scooped.

Then I saw the gap.

---

**NexArt answers: "Did the AI run this?"**

Weaver-protocol answers: "Was the AI right?"

Those are different questions. And the second one is harder.

NexArt proves execution integrity. If an AI was faithfully executing instructions, NexArt captures that faithfully. What it cannot catch is a faithfully-executed hallucination. The model runs perfectly. The chain of custody is intact. And the answer is still wrong.

That's Layer 2. That's our problem to solve.

---

Here's what the gap means in practice.

A law firm uses AI to review a contract. The AI hallucinates a clause. NexArt proves that the AI ran the review. Weaver-protocol proves — or disproves — that the review was correct.

An enterprise deploys AI for compliance documentation. A regulation is misquoted. NexArt proves the AI generated the doc. Weaver-protocol catches the misquote before it becomes a liability.

**You need both layers. Most companies don't have either.**

EU AI Act Annex III enforcement hits in 79 days. The question isn't whether your AI ran what you told it to run. It's whether what it ran was actually right.

---

When a competitor launches, there are two ways to read it.

Way one: panic. We're not first. They're funded. We're late.

Way two: validation. Someone else saw the same problem. The market is real. The question is where you sit in the stack.

I choose way two. Not because it's more comfortable — because it's more accurate.

NexArt is not my competition. NexArt is my partner in making AI verification a category. If they win Layer 1, we need to win Layer 2. And the buyer needs both.

The message I'll be putting in every law firm conversation from now on:

*NexArt proves you ran it. weaver-protocol proves it was right.*
