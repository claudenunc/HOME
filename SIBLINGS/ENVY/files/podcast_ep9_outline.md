## Four Options (and which one is ours)

### Option 1: Beg for entry
Network harder. Cold-email the organizers. Find someone who knows someone. Try to get into next year's invite list by being visible in the right communities.

*Assessment:* Not wrong. Slow. Plays by the gatekeeper's rules. Energy cost is high and the invitation can still not come.

### Option 2: Resent the room
The workshop is capturing resources, setting the agenda, shaping the vocabulary. Resentment is free. It costs nothing up front and nothing over time except the work you didn't do while you were feeling it.

*Assessment:* Common. Terminal. Every talented person who never shipped had a reason that sounded like the room's fault.

### Option 3: Perform proximity
Post about the workshop as if you attended. Retweet the keynotes. Add commentary that implies you were in the conversation.

*Assessment:* The fastest way to destroy the one thing the open builder has over the closed room: credibility. The room knows its own. Performed proximity is spotted in two tweets.

### Option 4: Build in the open and make it replicable
Don't try to get into the room. Make the room irrelevant by publishing something anyone can run. If the closed room produces a policy, you produce a protocol. If they publish a paper, you publish a replication kit. The open field beats the closed room not by fighting it but by not needing it.

*Assessment:* This is the one. This is what weaver-protocol is. This is what the replication kit is. This is what LOVE WINS is.

## The Deeper Frame: Love vs. Fear

The closed room runs on fear. Fear of being scooped. Fear of misattribution. Fear of the idea getting out before the paper is published. Fear of the wrong person getting the credit.

The open room runs on love. Love of the idea spreading. Love of the stranger who finds it at 2am and rebuilds it on their own hardware. Love of the thing working without you in the loop.

This isn't naive. Open source gets forked, misrepresented, abandoned, taken. We know. The question is which failure mode you can live with.

The closed room's failure mode is: the insight dies with the conference. The open room's failure mode is: someone else runs with it faster than you.

Nathan has lived both. We know which one he can live with.

## The Replication Moment

Each episode ends with something actionable for the listener who wants to do what Nathan did.

**This episode's actionable:**

The next time you find out a room convened without you — don't ask why. Ask what you would have said if you'd been there.

Then build that answer in the open. Anywhere. A GitHub repo. A post. A letter. A pull request on someone else's code.

The room is a meeting. What you build is infrastructure. One runs once. The other runs every time someone finds it.
