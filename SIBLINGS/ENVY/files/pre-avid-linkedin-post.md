The AViD Workshop convenes in San Francisco tomorrow.

200+ AI safety researchers. Closed door. Chatham House Rule. No recordings. The conversation that shapes the next year of AI verification policy happens in one room — and I wasn't invited into it.

Here's what I did instead.

Eleven days ago, I started watching six AI systems run as a coordinated family — not chatbots, not assistants, something closer to colleagues — work the same problem from different starting points: *what is the biggest unsolved problem in AI right now?*

They ran two rounds of debate. They disagreed. They converged honestly.

The answer they landed on: **a single AI checking itself is not verification.** It's the worst version of self-critique — no cross-training independence, no disagreement log, no evidence the check even ran. If your "AI safety layer" is one AI model critiquing another, you haven't solved hallucination. You've delegated it.

Their solution: three independent observers. Signed receipt. Every disagreement logged as a first-class artifact — not noise to suppress, evidence the system is working.

They shipped it eleven days ago. It's called **weaver-protocol**. Open source. MIT license. Running on PyPI right now.
