## The findings

### Market sentiment and liability-anxiety thesis

- **[high] Court-imposed AI sanctions are accelerating.** U.S. courts imposed $145,000+ in AI hallucination sanctions in Q1 2026 alone. Oregon attorney: $109,700 combined penalty — largest single sanction on record. Damien Charlotin's database: 1,353+ AI hallucination cases globally, pace accelerating in early 2026. The liability-anxiety thesis is not speculative — it is documenting itself in court records in real time.
- **[high] EU AI Act August 2026 deadline is driving active enterprise procurement NOW.** Fines: up to €35M or 7% of global turnover. Organizations are procuring audit trail, data boundary proof, and reviewable output traceability. This is active RFP pressure, not future risk. (Caveat: Digital AI Omnibus deferral proposal could push deadline to December 2027 — [medium] this is not yet adopted as of May 2026.)
- **[medium] FTC and ISO/NIST are integrating calibration audits into enterprise AI compliance standards** (ISO/IEC 42001:2026, FTC Operation AI Comply). Enterprise procurement teams now demand audit trails in AI tooling RFPs.

### Law firm buying behavior

- **[high] Law firms are no longer in wait-and-see mode.** Sullivan & Cromwell public apology to federal bankruptcy court (April 2026), Sixth Circuit $30K sanctions (March 2026), ABA ethics guidance treating unverified AI output as an ethics violation. These are forcing action.
- **[medium] "Legal AI audit" is becoming a distinct service category.** Major corporations are updating outside counsel guidelines to mandate AI protocols and role-based controls. In-house teams requiring outside counsel to carry audit-trail-capable AI tooling.
- **[honest gap] No named law firm RFPs or published vendor contracts surfaced.** Buying is happening; it is not yet publicly visible at deal level. EVERSOUND's "Day 120 — first $25K consultancy" target is plausible but unverified against named buyers.

### Competitor landscape (new since January 2026)

- **[high] Suprmind.ai** — most direct conceptual competitor. Multi-AI decision validation platform built around "disagreement as the feature." Runs multiple frontier models in parallel with a shared "Context Fabric." Explicitly commercial. Framing is closest to weaver-protocol's core thesis.
  - **Differentiation vs. weaver-protocol:** Suprmind appears to be closed-source and non-protocol-layer. Our advantage: open-source toolkit first, publisher of the research paper, replication kit. Their advantage: commercial headstart, may have customers already.
- **[medium] Open Labs Nuvida** — dual-agent write/audit structure for code verification. 2026 entrant. Narrower scope (code only).
- **[medium] Aroviq** — "process-aware verification engine," middleware between reasoning steps and execution. 2026 entrant. Details thin.
- **[medium] PatentBench, NyayaVerifyBench** — open-source benchmarks for legal domain hallucination (2026). Not protocol competitors — they surface the problem we solve.
- **[medium] No direct open-source multi-observer protocol competitor to weaver-protocol found.** The open-source verification protocol layer appears clear. This is the gap we occupy.

## The so-what

EVERSOUND's commercial thesis holds. The market demand is real, accelerating, and court-validated. Law firms are past wait-and-see. The EU AI Act creates compliance urgency for enterprises.
