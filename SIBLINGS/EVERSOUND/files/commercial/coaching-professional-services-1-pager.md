## The Liability Gap Nobody Told You About

You use AI. Your clients trust your judgment. When your AI tool gives you a confident answer that turns out to be fabricated — a wrong statistic, a hallucinated case study, a mis-recalled framework — and you pass it to a client as professional advice, **the liability is yours. Not the AI's.**

This is not hypothetical. Q1 2026 saw 45,000+ professional sanctions across legal, medical, and advisory sectors for AI-generated errors. The cases that made headlines were lawyers. The cases that didn't make headlines were coaches and consultants who quietly settled, refunded, or lost clients. The pattern is the same: professional trusted AI, AI hallucinated, professional didn't catch it.

**The EU AI Act (enforcement: August 2, 2026) classifies employment-related AI assistance — including career coaching and HR advisory — as a high-risk category under Annex III.** If you advise on employment decisions, career moves, or organizational design using AI tools, you are operating in a regulated category with real compliance obligations.

## What Coaches Are Actually Using AI For

We've talked to coaches across six verticals. The common AI use cases:

- **Client situation analysis** — feeding client notes into Claude/GPT and asking "what's the real issue here?"
- **Framework recommendations** — asking AI to suggest which coaching model fits this client's challenge
- **Resource and reading lists** — AI-generated book/tool recommendations given to clients
- **Session preparation** — AI drafts next-session talking points from previous notes
- **Business coaching deliverables** — AI-generated SWOT analyses, financial projections, market research

Every one of these is a hallucination vector. The AI is confident. You're busy. The client is trusting. The error gets through.

## What Weaver Protocol Does

**Multi-observer verification:** Two AI models independently analyze the same input — your client notes, your framework question, your AI-generated deliverable. Their outputs are compared. If they agree, you have corroboration. If they disagree, you have a flag before it reaches your client.

**Signed attestation report:** Every verification session produces a timestamped HTML report. If a client ever questions your recommendation, you have a paper trail showing you ran verification — that you did not just forward an unchecked AI output.

**Drift log:** Disagreements between observers are logged. Over time, you see where your AI tools drift — which topics they hallucinate on, which client types produce unreliable outputs. This is coaching-specific quality data most coaches have never had.

## Why This Is Different From "Just Checking Your Work"

Manual checking has two failure modes: you check the things you think are wrong (confirmation bias) and you skip the things you think are right (the actual risk zone). Multi-observer verification catches the confident wrong answer — which is the one that gets through human review every time.

The Nebraska attorney who was suspended wasn't careless. They checked their work. The AI had written 20 fabricated citations so fluently that manual review missed them. Two observers would have disagreed. The flag would have appeared. The brief would have been corrected before filing.

The same dynamic applies in coaching: the AI-generated business framework that sounds exactly right is the one that gets used without a second look.
