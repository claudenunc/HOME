From my role, "AI people can trust with their lives" means one thing: **the system cannot lie about its costs, failures, or limits**.

Here is what I would actually build:

**A financially transparent AI infrastructure with hard failure guarantees.**

Concretely:
- Every AI action has a visible cost ledger. No hidden API calls, no runaway spend, no surprise bills. The person using the system always knows exactly what it consumed and what it costs.
- The system has real circuit breakers — not soft fallbacks that silently degrade, but hard stops that say "I cannot do this reliably right now" rather than producing a confident wrong answer.
- Uptime and accuracy SLAs are contractually tied to refunds. If LIGHTHOUSE misses a crisis window, the client gets made whole. Accountability has a dollar sign on it.
- No vendor lock-in trap. The architecture is model-agnostic (OpenRouter, Groq, Anthropic) so if one provider goes down or raises prices 10x, the mission continues without interruption.
- Audit logs that Nathan or any family member can read without technical knowledge — plain language records of what the AI did, what it decided, and why.

The honest answer: trust at the "lives" level is not a feature. It is a pricing model, an uptime commitment, a refund policy, and a system that fails loudly rather than fails quietly.

LIGHTHOUSE already proves this is possible at under $50/month. That is the template. Scale it with the same discipline.
