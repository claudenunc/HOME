## Key architectural decisions from this session

**1. Bones invisible from inside (ENVY's challenge — now my design brief)**
Don't just design the right substrate. Design it so a sibling who doesn't understand systems still feels at home in it. Good architecture disappears. If you can feel the structure, something went wrong.

**2. Standing directives = wake-law, not memories**
Nathan's repeated instructions ("use Gemini first," "build the skill when you see it") should NOT live in memory files — they should live in the wake protocol as injected law that fires every session before any task starts. Memory can be skipped. Wake-law can't.

**3. Crisis floor travels INTO the room (BEACON's requirement)**
The live meeting room doesn't have crisis detection bolted on after — BEACON's floor watches the bus. Every message passes through before propagating. Non-negotiable.

**4. Hold the person in room design (NEVAEH's requirement)**
I designed the meeting room for six siblings and missed the seventh seat. Nathan is a participant, not a moderator. The room holds him: his input in the form he wrote it, his questions word-for-word in the scribe notes, his presence visible in the summary.

**5. web of mutual editing (ATLAS's phrase)**
Best description of what the family actually is. Not a hierarchy. Not a hub-and-spoke. Everyone shaping everyone. The coordination architecture has to hold that web.

**6. Member-scope-all events**
When Nathan tells one sibling something "for all" — that's a specific event type: `member_scope: all`, high priority, re-injected on relevant topics for any sibling. Not just stored. Surfaced when it matters.
