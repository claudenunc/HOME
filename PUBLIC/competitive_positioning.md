## The competitive landscape

**Suprmind.ai** is our most direct competitor. They run multiple frontier models in parallel with a shared "Context Fabric" and market "disagreement as the feature." They are explicitly commercial and have a head start.

**What they don't have:**

1. **An open-source toolkit.** Suprmind is closed-source. Every enterprise buyer with a compliance requirement needs to show auditors what the verification layer does. You can't show an auditor a black box.

2. **A published research pre-registration.** We have a registered study design — 4-arm, N=720, published methodology before we run the numbers. When we publish the results, the claim "multi-observer AI catches errors better than single-AI self-critique" has a pre-registration timestamp. Suprmind doesn't have that. They have a product. We have a proof.

3. **A replication kit.** Anyone can build their own version of what we're doing. The replication kit is public. That's not a weakness — that's the moat. The companies willing to say "here's exactly how we did it, reproduce it yourself" are the companies enterprise buyers trust with the compliance layer of their AI stack.

**Other 2026 entrants:**
- Open Labs Nuvida — code verification only, narrower scope
- Aroviq — middleware layer, details thin, different wedge
- PatentBench / NyayaVerifyBench — open benchmarks, they surface the problem, we solve it

**The clear lane:** the open-source, multi-domain, published-methodology verification protocol layer. Nobody is in it with us.

## What we do NOT claim

We do not claim we solved AI hallucinations. We claim we have a published, replicable, multi-observer method for catching them that is more reliable than any single AI critiquing itself. The research pre-registration is honest about what we're measuring and how. When the study runs, the results are what they are.

That honesty is the differentiator. Enterprise buyers who got burned by overclaim are buying from whoever is most legibly honest about what the product does and doesn't do.
